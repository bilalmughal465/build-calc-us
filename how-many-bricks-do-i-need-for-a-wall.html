How Many Bricks Do I Need for a Wall? A Comparison Table for Every Brick Size

One formula, six brick sizes compared side by side, a full worked example for a 20 ft x 8 ft wall, a table for every common wall size, and the mortar and sand maths — so you order once and do not run short.

Short answer

A 20 ft × 8 ft wall needs 1,080 standard bricks, or 1,188 bricks with 10% waste. That is a single-wythe wall in standard bricks (8 × 2.25 × 3.625 in) with a 3/8 in mortar joint. Plan for about 40 bags of 80 lb Type S mortar and about 8,000 lbs of mason sand. The quick rule is 6.75 standard bricks per square foot of wall, but the brick size changes the count a lot — the same wall needs 1,208 modular bricks, 812 king bricks, or only 528 utility bricks. Run your own numbers through the Brick Calculator, and use the Concrete Slab Calculator for the footing underneath the wall.

The formula: area, then bricks per square foot

Bricks = wall area in sq ft × bricks per sq ft for your brick size × number of wythes, then add 10% waste and round up. A wythe is one thickness of brick: a single skin (half-brick) garden wall is 1 wythe, a full structural wall is 2 wythes. The bricks-per-square-foot figure already includes the 3/8 in mortar joint, because the joint is part of the installed size of each brick — a standard brick covers 8.375 in × 2.625 in once the joint is added, which is 21.98 sq in, and 144 ÷ 21.98 = 6.75 bricks per sq ft.

The bigger the brick face, the fewer bricks you need: a utility brick covers more than twice the face of a standard brick, so the same wall needs less than half as many. But bigger bricks also mean fewer joints and a different look, so pick the size for the wall you want, not just the lowest count. Worked example: a 20 ft x 8 ft wall in standard brick

Step 1: measure the wall — 20 ft long, 8 ft high, no openings, standard bricks, single wythe. Step 2: work out the area — 20 × 8 = 160 sq ft. Step 3: multiply by bricks per sq ft — 160 × 6.75 = 1,080 bricks. Step 4: add 10% waste — 1,080 × 1.10 = 1,188 bricks to order. Step 5: mortar — about 1 bag of 80 lb Type S mortar per 30 bricks, so 1,188 ÷ 30 = 39.6, round up to 40 bags. Step 6: sand — about 200 lbs of mason sand per bag of mortar, so 40 × 200 = 8,000 lbs of sand. Step 7: cost planning — an installed brick wall typically costs about $15 to $30 per sq ft including materials and labour, so a 160 sq ft wall is roughly $2,400 to $4,800 installed. Confirm the price with your mason; treat the range as a planning figure, not a quote.

Second example: a small 10 ft x 4 ft garden wall

Area: 10 × 4 = 40 sq ft. Bricks: 40 × 6.75 = 270, and 270 × 1.10 = 297 bricks with waste. Mortar: 297 ÷ 30 = 9.9, so buy 10 bags of 80 lb Type S mortar. Sand: 10 × 200 = 2,000 lbs. A single-skin garden wall this size usually needs piers (thicker columns of brick) at intervals for stability, and a concrete strip footing below the frost line — count the pier bricks separately, about 2 bricks per course for a one-brick square pier.

Double-wythe walls: double the bricks

A double-wythe wall (one brick thick) is two interlocking skins, so it uses twice the bricks of a single skin. The same 20 ft × 8 ft wall needs 2,160 standard bricks before waste, or 2,376 bricks with 10% waste, and about 80 bags of mortar (2,376 ÷ 30 = 79.2, rounded up). The waste allowance is the same 10%, but the header courses that tie the two skins together add cutting, so allow 10% rather than 5% even on a straight wall. A double-wythe wall is the standard choice for a structural or load-bearing wall, and it looks finished from both sides.

Deducting door and window openings

Measure every opening, multiply width by height for its area, add the opening areas together, and subtract from the gross wall area before you multiply by bricks per sq ft. A standard 3 ft × 7 ft door is 21 sq ft, which saves 21 × 6.75 = 141.75, about 142 bricks. A standard 3 ft × 4 ft window is 12 sq ft, saving 12 × 6.75 = 81 bricks. A wall with two doors and three windows needs about 527 fewer bricks — skipping the deduction is how an order comes in a pallet too heavy. Do not deduct the opening until you have measured it: a door with a lintel course above it still needs the bricks over the opening, and the lintel itself is a separate material.

Mortar types and quantities

Mortar is not one product. Pick the type for where the wall sits, then count about 1 bag of 80 lb pre-mixed mortar per 30 standard bricks (about 33 bags per 1,000 bricks) and about 200 lbs of mason sand per bag.

Type S is the most common all-purpose choice for an exterior brick wall. Type N is the usual pick for an above-grade veneer or a soft brick that would crack under a harder mortar. Bonds, waste and the mistakes that cost bricks

The bond is the pattern the bricks are laid in, and it changes the waste. A running bond — each course offset by half a brick — is the most common, the strongest for a single skin, and the lowest waste at about 5% on a straight wall. English bond and Flemish bond alternate headers and stretchers and are used for one-brick walls; they need more cutting, so allow 7 to 10% waste. Decorative bonds, soldier courses (bricks standing on end), piers and corners all add bricks — count piers and soldier courses separately. The most common mistakes are the same ones in the common concrete mistakes guide: measuring the wrong thing, then trusting the maths. For bricks the classic errors are forgetting the mortar joint (using the brick size alone, which under-counts by about 5%), forgetting the second wythe, and forgetting to deduct openings — or deducting them and then forgetting the lintel bricks above them.

Method and limits

This guide counts bricks by wall area (length × height in sq ft, openings subtracted) multiplied by the bricks per sq ft for the brick size (standard 6.75, modular 6.86, queen 5.76, king 4.61, utility 3.00, engineer 5.63), multiplied by the number of wythes, with 10% added for waste and every count rounded up to whole bricks. The per-sq-ft figures assume a 3/8 in mortar joint in a running bond; king and queen sizes vary by manufacturer, so confirm the nominal size with your supplier. Mortar is estimated at about 1 bag of 80 lb pre-mixed Type S per 30 standard bricks and sand at about 200 lbs per bag of mortar — confirm both with your supplier, because mortar use varies with joint thickness and brick type. The installed cost range ($15 to $30 per sq ft) is a typical US planning range, not a quote, and varies by region, mason and season. Structural walls, retaining walls and walls over about 4 ft tall usually need proper foundations and design — check with your local building department. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final order with your supplier before you buy. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how many bricks I need for a wall?

Multiply wall length by wall height to get the area in square feet, subtract door and window openings, multiply by the bricks per square foot for your brick size (6.75 for a standard brick with a 3/8 in mortar joint), multiply by the number of wythes (1 for a single skin, 2 for a double skin), then add 10% waste and round up. A 20 ft x 8 ft wall is 160 sq ft, so 160 x 6.75 = 1,080 standard bricks, 1,188 with waste.

How many bricks do I need for a 20x8 wall?

A 20 ft x 8 ft wall needs 1,080 standard bricks (8 x 2.25 x 3.625 in), or 1,188 bricks with 10% waste, for a single-wythe wall. Plan for about 40 bags of 80 lb Type S mortar (about 1 bag per 30 bricks) and about 8,000 lbs of mason sand. A double-wythe wall of the same size needs 2,376 bricks with waste and about 80 bags of mortar.

How many bricks are there per square foot of wall?

With a 3/8 in mortar joint, a single-wythe wall needs about 6.75 standard bricks per square foot, 6.86 modular bricks, 5.76 queen bricks, 4.61 king bricks, 3.00 utility bricks and 5.63 engineer bricks. The bigger the brick face, the fewer bricks per square foot. Always use the nominal size plus the joint, because the joint is part of the installed size.

How much mortar do I need for 1,000 bricks?

About 33 bags of 80 lb pre-mixed Type S mortar per 1,000 standard bricks — about 1 bag per 30 bricks — plus about 200 lbs of mason sand per bag of mortar. A 20 ft x 8 ft wall (1,188 bricks with waste) needs about 40 bags of mortar and about 8,000 lbs of sand. Mortar use varies with joint thickness and brick type, so check the bag and confirm with your supplier.

What is the difference between a standard brick and a modular brick?

A standard brick measures 8 x 2.25 x 3.625 in and needs 6.75 bricks per square foot; a modular brick measures 7.625 x 2.25 x 3.625 in and needs 6.86 per square foot. The modular brick is slightly shorter, so the same 20 ft x 8 ft wall needs 1,208 modular bricks with waste instead of 1,188 standard bricks. Modular size is the more common size in new US construction.

How many bricks do I need for a double-wythe wall?

Double the single-wythe count. A 20 ft x 8 ft wall in standard brick needs 2,160 bricks before waste, or 2,376 bricks with 10% waste, and about 80 bags of 80 lb mortar. A double-wythe (one-brick thick) wall is two interlocking skins, so it uses twice the bricks of a single skin, plus extra waste for the header courses that tie the two skins together.
