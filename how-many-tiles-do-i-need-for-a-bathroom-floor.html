How Many Tiles Do I Need for a Bathroom Floor? A Worked Project Walkthrough

A full materials list for a real 8 ft × 10 ft bathroom — tiles, boxes, thinset and grout — worked out step by step, so you can check your own order before you pay for it.

Short answer

An 8 ft × 10 ft bathroom floor with 12×12 in tiles and 1/8 in grout joints needs 79 tiles to cover it, 87 tiles with a 10% waste allowance, which is 8 boxes of 12 tiles — plus 2 bags of 50 lb thinset and 1 bag of 25 lb grout. The grout joint matters: each tile covers 1.02 sq ft once the joint is counted, not 1 sq ft, so the bare count is 79, not 80. The formula

Area to tile = length (ft) × width (ft). Area per tile (sq ft) = (tile width + grout joint) × (tile length + grout joint) ÷ 144, all in inches. Tiles to cover = area to tile ÷ area per tile, rounded up. Tiles to buy = tiles to cover × (1 + waste %), rounded up — 10% for a straight grid, 15% for diagonal, 20% for herringbone. Boxes = tiles to buy ÷ tiles per box, rounded up. Tile is sold by the box, so a partial box is still opened and paid for. Use the actual tile size from the box, not the nominal size: a 300 × 300 mm tile is really 11.81 in, and with a 1/8 in joint it takes about 3% more tiles than a true 12 in tile.

Worked example: 8 ft × 10 ft bathroom floor, 12×12 in tiles

Step 1: the area is 8 × 10 = 80 sq ft. Step 2: each tile with its 1/8 in joint covers (12 + 0.125) × (12 + 0.125) ÷ 144 = 12.125 × 12.125 ÷ 144 = 1.02 sq ft. Step 3: the bare tile count is 80 ÷ 1.02 = 78.4, rounded up to 79 tiles. Step 4: with a 10% waste allowance for a straight layout, 79 × 1.10 = 86.9, rounded up to 87 tiles to buy. Step 5: in boxes of 12, 87 ÷ 12 = 7.25, rounded up to 8 boxes, which is 96 tiles — 9 spare tiles for cuts, breakage and future repairs. Step 6: thinset, at about 60 sq ft per 50 lb bag with a 1/4 × 3/8 in notched trowel, is 80 ÷ 60 = 1.33, rounded up to 2 bags. Step 7: sanded grout is about 6 lb for this floor, so one 25 lb bag covers it. If your own numbers are far from these for a bathroom this size, check the waste allowance first — skipping it is the most common reason an order comes up short.

What changes with a different tile size or layout

With 12×24 in tiles on the same 8 × 10 ft floor, each tile with its joint covers (12.125 × 24.125) ÷ 144 = 2.03 sq ft, so the bare count is 80 ÷ 2.03 = 39.4, rounded up to 40 tiles, and 44 tiles with 10% waste — 6 boxes of 8. Fewer, bigger tiles mean fewer joints and fewer cuts at the walls, but each tile is heavier and the floor must be flatter. The layout matters as much as the size: a diagonal layout cuts tiles at an angle along all four walls, and herringbone cuts at every course, so both waste far more than a straight grid.

No standard sets these percentages — they are common rules of thumb, and some suppliers recommend more, especially for natural stone and large-format tile that cannot easily be reordered. Thinset and grout for the same bathroom

Thinset coverage depends on the trowel notch, not on the tile count. A 50 lb bag covers about 60 sq ft with a 1/4 × 3/8 in square-notch trowel, about 90–100 sq ft with a 1/4 × 1/4 in notch, and about 42–47 sq ft with a 1/2 × 1/2 in notch, per the manufacturer's data sheet — so this 80 sq ft floor needs 2 bags with the standard notch. Grout fills the joints, so the amount depends on joint width, tile size and tile thickness: about 6 lb of sanded grout for this floor, which one 25 lb bag covers comfortably. Use non-sanded grout for joints up to 1/8 in and sanded grout for joints from 1/8 to 1/2 in. Both figures are estimates — check the coverage printed on the bag you are actually buying, and remember that back-buttering large tiles uses extra thinset on top of the trowelled bed.

Walls and showers are a separate calculation

Bathrooms are usually the one room where floor and wall both get tiled, and each needs its own count. Measure each wall separately — width × height up to where the tile stops — and add them together, subtracting large windows and doors. A 5 ft back wall and two 32 in side walls tiled 8 ft high come to about 83 sq ft, and the shower floor is measured on its own because it usually takes a smaller mosaic tile. Use the Shower Tile Calculator for the shower itself. Behind wall tile in tub and shower areas, only the backer materials the building code allows may be used — cement backer board, not standard drywall — so measure the wall area for backer board separately as well.

Method and limits

This guide counts tiles for a rectangular bathroom floor: area divided by the effective tile area including the grout joint, rounded up, plus a waste allowance by layout, then rounded up to full boxes. It assumes one tile size across the floor, a straight grid unless the layout table says otherwise, and measurements taken wall to wall. Irregular rooms should be split into rectangles and added, with 15% waste for the extra cuts. Waste percentages are common rules of thumb, not a standard — no ANSI or TCNA standard sets an overage. Tile sizes are nominal: check the real size on the box, because a 300 × 300 mm tile is 11.81 in, not 12 in. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final order with your supplier before you buy. Frequently asked questions

How many 12x12 tiles do I need for a 5x8 bathroom floor?

A 5x8 ft bathroom floor is 40 sq ft. With 12x12 in tiles and 1/8 in grout joints that is 40 tiles to cover it, or 44 tiles with a 10% waste allowance, which is 4 boxes of 12 tiles.

How much extra tile should I buy?

Buy about 10% extra for a straight grid or brick-offset layout, 15% for a diagonal layout and 20% for herringbone. No standard sets the overage, so these are common rules of thumb. Round up to full boxes, and buy one spare box on top if you want tiles kept back for future repairs, because dye lots vary between production runs.

Does the grout joint change how many tiles I need?

Yes, slightly. A 1/8 in grout joint makes each 12x12 in tile cover 1.02 sq ft instead of 1 sq ft, so an 80 sq ft floor needs 79 tiles bare instead of 80. The effect is bigger on small tiles: a 1/4 in joint on a 3x6 in subway tile takes up about 11% of the wall, so leaving it out would have you buying too much tile.

How many boxes of tile do I need?

Divide the tile count, after the waste allowance, by the tiles per box printed on the box label, and round up. For an 8x10 ft bathroom that is 87 tiles divided by 12 tiles per box, which is 7.25, so 8 boxes. Tile is sold by the box, so a partial box still has to be opened and paid for.

How much grout and thinset do I need for an 80 sq ft bathroom floor?

For an 80 sq ft floor with 12x12 in tiles, allow 2 bags of 50 lb thinset, at about 60 sq ft per bag with a 1/4 by 3/8 in notched trowel, and about 6 lb of sanded grout, so one 25 lb bag covers it. Check the coverage printed on the bag you are actually buying, because trowel size and joint width change both figures.

How do I calculate tiles for an odd-shaped bathroom?

Split the floor into rectangles, measure each one separately, and add the areas together. Include closets and the area under a freestanding tub or vanity if the tile goes there. Use a 15% waste allowance instead of 10%, because the extra corners and cuts around fixtures produce more offcuts.
