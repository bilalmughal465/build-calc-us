How Much Carpet Do I Need for a Room? A Worked Project Walkthrough

A 12x14 ft bedroom worked from tape measure to square yards to pad to tack strips to cost — the square-yard formula, the waste allowance, the 12 ft roll seam trap that doubles the order on a wide room, the stairs maths, and the carpet-vs-hard-flooring decision, with every number shown so you can check it against your own room.

Short answer

A 12 ft × 14 ft bedroom needs 21 square yards of carpet to order — 168 sq ft of floor, 18.67 sq yd net, plus 10% waste. Order the same 19 sq yd of pad, about 49 ft of tack strips, and at the example prices in this guide the whole job costs about $770. Work the walkthrough for your own room, or size the order with the Carpet Calculator. The full flooring calculators list covers the carpet, the pad and the rest of the floor job.

The walkthrough: carpeting a 12 ft × 14 ft bedroom

Step 1 — Measure the room. Measure length and width wall to wall at the longest and widest points, in feet, including the closet and the doorway area the carpet will cover. This bedroom is 12 ft × 14 ft. An L-shaped room is two rectangles — measure each one separately and add the areas together.

Step 2 — Work out the net area. 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft. Carpet is sold by the square yard, and there are 9 sq ft in a square yard, so 168 ÷ 9 = 18.67 sq yd net. That is the floor area only — nobody orders the net figure, because cutting and trimming eat part of every roll.

Step 3 — Add the waste allowance. This is a plain rectangle, so 10%: 168 × 1.10 = 184.8 sq ft, and 184.8 ÷ 9 = 20.53 sq yd — round up to 21 sq yd to order. The waste covers the trim at the walls, the cut at the doorway and the odd damaged piece. Rooms with angles, closets or stairs need 15% or more.

Step 4 — Order the pad by the room area, not the carpet order. Pad is sold by the square yard too, but it is laid wall to wall without roll-width waste, so it follows the room's net area: 168 sq ft is 18.67 sq yd, so order 19 sq yd of pad — not the 21 sq yd of carpet. Size it with the Carpet Pad Calculator. Skimping on pad to save money is a false economy: thin or missing pad is what makes a new carpet feel flat within a year.

Step 5 — Work out the tack strips. Tack strips run around the perimeter, minus the doorways: 2 × (12 + 14) = 52 ft, less a 3 ft doorway = 49 ft. Tack strips are cheap — about $0.50 per ft at the example price — so this is about $25, but forgetting them is the classic last-minute trip back to the shop.

Step 6 — Check the roll width before you order. Carpet comes off a 12 ft wide roll, and the installer needs about 3 in of trim at each wall. This room is 12 ft wide, so the cut is 12 ft 6 in across with trim — wider than the roll — which means a second strip and a seam. Read the seam trap section below before you pay: on a 12 ft roll this room can need far more than 21 sq yd, while a 15 ft roll covers it with one piece.

Step 7 — Price the whole job. At the example prices — $25 per sq yd carpet, $5 per sq yd pad, $6 per sq yd installation, $0.50 per ft tack strips — the job is 21 × $25 = $525 carpet, 19 × $5 = $95 pad, 21 × $6 = $126 installation and 49 × $0.50 = $25 tack strips, for a total of about $770. These are example prices, not a quote — get a price from your local supplier before you buy.

Cross-check with a second room: a 14 ft × 11 ft bedroom is 154 sq ft, which is 17.1 sq yd net, and with 10% waste that is 18.8 sq yd — order 19 sq yd. Other carpet calculators publish 19.3 sq yd for the same room using the strip model with wall trim, so the two methods agree within half a yard — the difference is the trim, not an error. The 12 ft roll seam trap

Carpet comes in standard roll widths of 12 ft, and occasionally 15 ft. The Carpet and Rug Institute installation standard (CRI 105) calls for at least 3 in of carpet along each wall for trimming, so a room that measures exactly 12 ft wide needs a cut 12 ft 6 in wide — wider than the roll. That forces a second strip and a seam, and the offcut from the second strip is mostly wasted.

On a 12 ft × 12 ft room, the waste method says 18 sq yd, but the strip model says 33.33 sq yd on a 12 ft roll (two strips, one seam) against 20.83 sq yd on a 15 ft roll (one strip, no seam). The same room, the same carpet — nearly twice the material — decided by the roll width alone. Before you order a room wider than about 11 ft 6 in, ask your installer for a seaming diagram and ask your supplier whether the carpet comes in a 15 ft roll. The waste percentage is a planning figure; the strip plan is what you actually pay for.

How much carpet do stairs need?

Stairs are a separate calculation. Each step has a tread (the part you step on, about 10 in deep) and a riser (the vertical face, about 7 in tall), so each step needs about 17 in of carpet. Measure the tread plus the riser, multiply by the stair width and the number of steps, then add 20% waste.

Worked example: a 36 in wide staircase with 13 steps needs 17 in × 13 steps × 36 in = 7,956 sq in, which is 55.25 sq ft. Add 20% waste and that is 66.3 sq ft, or 7.37 sq yd — order 8 sq yd for the stairs. Carpet the stairs from the same roll as the room where you can, so the colour and batch match.

Carpet vs hard flooring: which one fits the room

Carpet is warm underfoot, quiet and cheap per square yard, and it suits bedrooms and living rooms. It stains, it holds dust and allergens, and it wears visibly on the traffic paths, so it is a poor choice for kitchens, bathrooms and entryways. Size the hard-flooring side of the decision with the tile guide and the flooring calculators.

Hard flooring — tile, laminate or hardwood — costs more per square foot and feels colder, but it lasts longer, cleans easily and suits wet rooms. If the room is a bathroom or a kitchen, carpet is not the answer at all: tile or vinyl is. If the room is a bedroom and the budget is tight, carpet usually wins on price and comfort.

Either way, the subfloor decides the result. Carpet laid on a damp or uneven subfloor wears badly and can smell within months. Fix squeaks, level the dips and check for damp before the carpet goes down — the cheapest carpet on a good subfloor outlasts expensive carpet on a bad one.

What changes the answer for carpet

Your room shape. A rectangle is length × width, but an L-shaped room is two rectangles added together, and a room with bay windows or alcoves needs each section measured separately.

Your roll width. The area to order does not change, but the strip count does. A room wider than about 11 ft 6 in needs a seam on a 12 ft roll, and that seam can nearly double the order — ask for the seaming plan before you pay.

Your waste allowance. 10% for a plain rectangle, 15% for seams, angles and pattern matching, 20% or more for stairs. Skipping it entirely is the single most common reason a room comes up short.

Your pattern. A patterned carpet has to line up at every seam, so the installer cuts more off each piece to match the pattern. Plain carpet wastes the least; a large repeating pattern wastes the most.

Your pad. Pad is ordered by the room's net square yards, rounded up — 19 sq yd here, not the carpet's 21 — because pad has no roll-width waste, but its thickness and density change the feel and the price. Cheap thin pad under good carpet is money wasted twice — once on the carpet, once on replacing it early.

Common mistakes that leave a room short

Ordering the net area with no waste allowance. Every wall needs trim and every seam needs matching. 10% to 15% extra is standard practice, not padding.

Measuring the room, not the carpet. Closets, alcoves and doorways the carpet will cover are part of the order. Measure wall to wall at the longest and widest points, or the offcuts will not cover the corners.

Forgetting the roll width. A 12 ft wide room on a 12 ft roll needs a second strip. The waste percentage does not catch this — only the strip plan does.

Rounding down. Carpet is sold by the square yard and cut from a roll. 20.53 sq yd is an order for 21 sq yd, not 20 — and the spare yard is the piece you reach for when a cut goes wrong.

Skipping the pad. Pad is not optional trim — it is what the carpet sits on. Ordering carpet without pad, or reusing flat old pad under new carpet, shortens the new carpet's life.

Method and limits

This guide calculates carpet as area = length × width for a rectangle, square yards = area ÷ 9, area to order = net area × (1 + waste ÷ 100), and square yards to order = area to order ÷ 9, rounded up — the same formula the BuildCalc Carpet Calculator runs. The standard roll width is 12 ft, with 15 ft rolls available for some ranges, and the Carpet and Rug Institute standard (CRI 105) calls for at least 3 in of carpet along each wall for trimming. The waste allowance is 10% for a plain rectangle, 15% for seams, angles, L-shaped rooms and patterned carpet, and 20% or more for stairs. Pad is ordered at the room's net square yards, rounded up, because pad is laid wall to wall without roll-width waste. The $25 per sq yd carpet, $5 per sq yd pad, $6 per sq yd installation and $0.50 per ft tack strip figures are the guide's example prices, not quoted market prices, and carpet prices vary a lot by fibre, quality, region and supplier. The strip-model figures (33.33 sq yd for a 12 × 12 ft room on a 12 ft roll) come from the roll-width method other carpet calculators publish, and they show why a seaming plan matters on wide rooms. This guide counts carpet, pad, tack strips and installation only: it does not price furniture moving, old carpet removal and disposal, subfloor repair, transitions or thresholds, and it does not cover which fibre suits your household, pets or allergies. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final order with your supplier and installer before you buy. Frequently asked questions

How much carpet do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12 ft by 12 ft room is 144 sq ft, which is 16 sq yd net. Add 10% waste and you order 17.6 sq yd — round up to 18 sq yd. But check the roll width first: carpet comes off a 12 ft roll, and with 3 in of trim at each wall a 12 ft wide room needs a second strip, so ask your installer for a seaming plan before you order — the strip count can change the order a lot.

How do you calculate square yards of carpet?

Multiply the room length by the width in feet to get square feet, divide by 9 to get square yards, then multiply by 1 plus the waste percentage. A 12 ft by 14 ft room is 168 sq ft, which is 18.67 sq yd net, and with 10% waste it is 20.53 sq yd — order 21 sq yd.

How much waste should I add for carpet?

Add 10% for a plain rectangular room, 15% for a room wider than the 12 ft roll, an L-shaped room or a patterned carpet, and 20% or more for stairs. The waste covers trimming at the walls, seams, pattern matching and the odd damaged piece — ordering the net area leaves the room short.

How much carpet pad do I need?

Order the pad by the room's floor area, not the carpet order. A 12 ft by 14 ft room is 168 sq ft, which is 18.67 sq yd net, so order 19 sq yd of pad. Pad is laid wall to wall without roll-width waste, so it does not need the carpet's waste allowance.

How much does it cost to carpet a 12x14 bedroom?

These are example prices, not a quote, so get a price from your local supplier before you buy.

What is the standard roll width for carpet?
