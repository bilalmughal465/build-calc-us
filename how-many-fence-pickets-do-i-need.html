How Many Fence Pickets Do I Need?

One formula, then a comparison table for every common picket width and gap — so you can read off your count instead of guessing it.

Short answer

A 150 ft fence with 5.5 inch pickets and a 1/4 inch gap needs 314 pickets — 346 once a 10% waste allowance is added. The count comes from one rule: each picket covers its own width plus the gap beside it. That combined width is called the pitch. Divide the fence run, in inches, by the pitch, round up, and add waste. Try the Fence Picket Calculator for your own run, or read your count off the table below.

The formula

Pitch = picket width + gap, in inches. Pickets = fence run in inches ÷ pitch, rounded up. Pickets to buy = pickets × 1.10 (10% waste), rounded up. Posts at 8 ft spacing = (fence length ÷ 8, rounded up) + 1. Rails at 2 per section = sections × 2. Screws at 2 per picket per rail = pickets × rails per section × 2. Always use the actual board width: a nominal 1x6 is 5.5 inches, and a nominal 1x4 is 3.5 inches.

Worked example: 150 ft fence, 5.5 inch pickets, 1/4 inch gap

Step 1: the pitch is 5.5 + 0.25 = 5.75 inches. Step 2: the run in inches is 150 × 12 = 1,800 inches. Step 3: pickets are 1,800 ÷ 5.75 = 313.04, rounded up to 314. Step 4: with 10% waste, 314 × 1.10 = 345.4, rounded up to 346 pickets to buy. Step 5: posts at 8 ft spacing are (150 ÷ 8 = 18.75, rounded up to 19 sections) + 1 = 20 posts. Step 6: rails at 2 per section are 19 × 2 = 38 rails. Step 7: screws at 2 per picket per rail are 314 × 2 × 2 = 1,256 screws — order boxes, not bags. If this fence has a 4 ft gate, subtract it first: the picket run becomes 146 ft, which is 1,752 ÷ 5.75 = 304.69, rounded up to 305 pickets, 336 with waste. The gate posts are counted with the posts, not the pickets.

Method and limits

This guide counts pickets for a straight fence run: run in inches divided by picket width plus gap, rounded up, plus a 10% waste allowance for straight layouts. It assumes pickets of one width, one gap, and 8 ft post spacing for the post and rail counts in the worked example. Corners, slopes, steps, short end sections and board-on-board overlap change the count, and shadowbox counts use the common 1.5x rule of thumb rather than a per-board layout. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final list with your supplier before you order. Frequently asked questions

How many fence pickets do I need for a 100 ft fence?

With 5.5 inch pickets and a 1/4 inch gap, a 100 ft fence needs 209 pickets, 230 with 10% waste. A tight privacy fence with no gap needs 219 pickets, 241 with waste, and a classic picket fence with 3.5 inch boards and a 2.5 inch gap needs 200 pickets, 220 with waste.

Is a 1x6 fence board really 6 inches wide?

No. A nominal 1x6 board is dressed to 5.5 inches wide, and a nominal 1x4 board is 3.5 inches wide. Count with the actual width plus the gap, not the nominal size, or your picket count comes out short.

How many pickets go in one 8 ft fence section?

An 8 ft section is 96 inches. With 5.5 inch pickets and a 1/4 inch gap it takes 17 pickets (96 divided by 5.75, rounded up). A tight privacy section of the same boards takes 18, and a classic picket section with 5.5 inch boards and a 3.5 inch gap takes 11.

Do I subtract the gate width before counting pickets?

Yes. Subtract the full gate width from the fence run before you divide by picket width plus gap, because no pickets go across the gate opening. Count the gate posts separately: the posts on both sides of the gate are part of the post count, not the picket count.

How many extra pickets should I buy for waste?

Add 10% for a straight run, and 15% for a fence with corners, angles, slopes or many short sections. Cuts, end pieces, split boards and one bad board all eat pickets that a bare count does not include.

How many more pickets does a shadowbox or board-on-board fence need?

About 1.5 times as many as a single-sided privacy fence over the same run, because boards go on both sides of the rails with an overlap. A 100 ft run that needs 219 single-sided 5.5 inch pickets needs about 329 boards for a shadowbox or board-on-board build, 362 with 10% waste.
