How Many Deck Boards Do I Need?

The 6 counting mistakes that leave a deck a row short — with a worked 16 ft x 12 ft example, the formula, and a size table.

Short answer

A 16 ft × 12 ft deck needs 26 rows of 5.5 inch boards with a 1/4 inch gap — 416 linear feet, or 29 boards of 16 ft once a 10% waste allowance is added. The count comes from one rule: each board covers its own width plus the gap beside it. Skip that rule and the deck comes up short. Work through the mistakes below with your own size, or try the Deck Board Calculator for an instant result.

The formula

Coverage per board = board width + gap, in inches. Rows = deck width in inches ÷ coverage per board, rounded up. Linear feet = rows × board length in feet. Boards to buy = rows × 1.10 (10% waste), rounded up — using boards as long as the row, so one board per row. Joists at 16 inches on center = (run in inches ÷ 16) + 1. Screws = rows × joists × 2, at two screws per board per joist.

Worked example: 16 ft × 12 ft deck, boards run the 16 ft length

Step 1: coverage per board is 5.5 + 0.25 = 5.75 inches. Step 2: rows across the 12 ft width are 144 ÷ 5.75 = 25.04, rounded up to 26 rows — the last row is ripped narrower to fit. Step 3: linear feet are 26 × 16 = 416 ft. Step 4: with 10% waste, 26 × 1.10 = 28.6, rounded up to 29 boards of 16 ft. Step 5: joists at 16 inches on center across the 16 ft run are (192 ÷ 16) + 1 = 13 joists. Step 6: screws at two per board per joist are 26 × 13 × 2 = 676 screws — order a box, not a bag.

The 6 mistakes

1. Counting the nominal width, not the actual width. A nominal 6 inch board is dressed to 5.5 inches. Counting 6 inches across a 12 ft width gives 24 rows; the real count is 26. Two rows short, before you start.

2. Forgetting the gap entirely. A 1/4 inch gap across 26 boards adds 6.5 inches of width — more than a whole board. Ignore it and the last row does not fit, or the deck ends up wider than the frame.

3. Ordering the bare row count, with no waste. Cuts, end pieces, a ripped last row and one defective board all eat stock. A straight rectangular deck needs 10% extra; angles, curves, stairs or a picture-frame border need 15% to 20%.

4. Running the boards the wrong way for your stock length. On this deck, rows along the 16 ft side need 26 rows from 16 ft stock. Rows along the 12 ft side need 34 rows across the 16 ft width — 34 boards of 16 ft stock, each cut down, against 29 the other way. Check direction against the lengths you can actually buy.

5. Spacing joists for lumber when you are laying composite, or for square when you are laying diagonal. 16 inches on center suits most lumber run perpendicular to the joists. Diagonal decking and many composite boards need 12 inches on center, because composite spans are often shorter than lumber. Check the manufacturer's span table, not a rule of thumb.

6. Forgetting fasteners, and the frame under the surface. Two screws per board per joist runs into four figures on even a modest deck — 676 on the example above — and hidden-clip systems have their own coverage rating per square foot. Boards are only the surface: joists, beams, posts and footings are a separate count, and footings are the genuinely structural part. In most places a deck attached to the house or raised above a certain height needs a permit, so check with your local building department before you build.

Method and limits

This guide counts boards for a flat, rectangular deck: rows from the actual board width plus the gap, rounded up, plus a 10% waste allowance for straight layouts. It assumes one board per row (stock at least as long as the row), two screws per board per joist, and joists at 16 inches on center for lumber run perpendicular. Diagonal layouts, composite spans, picture-frame borders, stairs, railings and footings change the count, so treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final list with your supplier and your local code. Frequently asked questions

How many deck boards do I need for a 16 ft x 12 ft deck?

With 5.5 inch boards and a 1/4 inch gap, a 16 ft x 12 ft deck needs 26 rows of 16 ft boards, which is 416 linear feet. With a 10% waste allowance, buy 29 boards of 16 ft.

What gap should I leave between deck boards?

About 1/4 inch for wet pressure-treated wood, which shrinks slightly as it dries, and about 1/8 inch for kiln-dried wood. Composite decking follows the manufacturer's specified gap for thermal expansion, so check the product instructions before you count.

How far apart should deck joists be?

16 inches on center is standard for most decking run perpendicular to the joists. Drop to 12 inches on center for diagonal decking or for composite boards, many of which specify a shorter span than lumber. Always check the decking manufacturer's span table.

Should I add extra boards for waste?

Yes. Add 10% for a straight rectangular deck, and 15% to 20% for decks with angles, curves, stairs or a picture-frame border. Cuts, end pieces, defects and one ripped last row all eat boards that a bare row count does not include.

Is a 6 inch deck board really 6 inches wide?

No. A nominal 6 inch board is dressed to 5.5 inches wide, and a nominal 4 inch board is 3.5 inches wide. Count with the actual width plus the gap, not the nominal size, or your row count comes out short.

Do I need a permit to build a deck?

In most places, yes, particularly for a deck attached to the house or raised above a certain height. Footings, ledger attachment and railings are the parts inspectors look at hardest, so check with your local building department before you build.
