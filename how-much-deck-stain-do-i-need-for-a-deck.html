How Much Deck Stain Do I Need for a Deck? Formula Reference With Worked Examples

A deck is more than its floor — the railings and stairs often add as much surface again. The full formula, the coverage rates by stain type, the worked example for a 16x20 ft deck, the deck-size tables and the two mistakes that leave a deck half-stained, with every number shown so you can check it against your own deck.

Short answer

A 16 ft × 20 ft deck with 48 ft of railing and 5 steps needs 3.74 gallons of stain for two coats — buy 4 one-gallon cans. That is 567.25 sq ft of surface (320 floor + 216 railing + 31.25 stairs) at 250 sq ft per gallon for the first coat and 500 for the second, plus 10% extra. At the example price of $45 per gallon the stain costs about $180. Work the formula for your own deck, or size the order with the Deck Stain Calculator. The full deck calculators list covers the stain, the boards and the rest of the deck job.

The formula

Total area = floor area + railing area + stair area

Floor area = deck length × deck width

Railing area = railing length × 4.5 sq ft per ft (baluster railing; or length × height × 2 for both faces)

Stair area = steps × stair width × (11.25 + 7.5) ÷ 12 (tread plus riser, in feet)

First coat (gal) = total area ÷ first-coat coverage

Second coat (gal) = total area ÷ second-coat coverage

To buy = (first coat + second coat) × 1.10, rounded up to whole cans

Four steps, in this order. First, add up every surface the stain has to cover — floor, railings and stairs — because a can label quotes coverage per gallon, not per deck. Second, divide by the first-coat coverage from your can: the first coat soaks in, so it covers less. Third, divide by the second-coat coverage, which goes about twice as far because the wood is already sealed. Fourth, add 10% for board edges, gaps and touch-ups, and round up to whole cans — a half-empty can at the end of the job is cheaper than a second trip to the shop mid-coat.

The worked example: a 16 ft × 20 ft deck, two coats

A 16 ft × 20 ft deck with 48 ft of baluster railing and 5 steps, 4 ft wide, stained with a semi-transparent stain on smooth wood: 250 sq ft per gallon first coat, 500 second coat, 10% extra.

Step 1 — Floor. 16 × 20 = 320 sq ft.

Step 2 — Railings. A baluster railing has four sides on every baluster plus the top and bottom rails, so it counts far more surface than its outline: 48 × 4.5 = 216 sq ft. That is nearly as much as the floor — the railing is the part most people forget.

Step 3 — Stairs. Each step is an 11.25 in tread plus a 7.5 in riser, 18.75 in in total: 5 × 4 × 18.75 ÷ 12 = 31.25 sq ft.

Step 4 — Total area and gallons. 320 + 216 + 31.25 = 567.25 sq ft. First coat: 567.25 ÷ 250 = 2.27 gal. Second coat: 567.25 ÷ 500 = 1.13 gal. Together 3.40 gal, plus 10% extra: 3.40 × 1.10 = 3.74 gal — buy 4 one-gallon cans.

Step 5 — Price it. At the example price of $45 per gallon, 4 cans cost $180; add about $17 of deck cleaner and the materials come to about $197. A professional job at the example $1.50 per sq ft labour rate is 567.25 × $1.50 = about $851 of labour, so about $1,050 all in with materials and cleaner. These are example prices, not a quote — get a price from your local supplier before you buy.

Cross-check with a smaller deck: a 12 ft × 16 ft deck with 36 ft of railing and 3 steps, 4 ft wide, is 192 + 162 + 18.75 = 372.75 sq ft. Two coats at 250 then 500 sq ft per gallon: 372.75 ÷ 250 = 1.49 gal first coat, 372.75 ÷ 500 = 0.75 gal second coat, 2.24 gal together, plus 10% = 2.46 gal — buy 3 cans. Rough or weathered wood: the same deck

Old, weathered boards are more porous, so the same 16 ft × 20 ft deck drinks far more stain: at 150 sq ft per gallon first coat and 300 second coat, the 567.25 sq ft needs 567.25 ÷ 150 = 3.78 gal first coat and 567.25 ÷ 300 = 1.89 gal second coat, 5.67 gal together, plus 10% = 6.24 gal — buy 7 one-gallon cans, or a 5-gallon pail plus 2 cans if your store sells one for less. At the example $45 per gallon that is $315 of stain, against $180 on smooth wood. If your boards are grey and rough, budget for the rough-wood column, not the smooth one.

Two mistakes that leave a deck half-stained

Counting the floor only. On the worked deck the railing (216 sq ft) and stairs (31.25 sq ft) add 247.25 sq ft to a 320 sq ft floor — nearly 80% more surface. A floor-only order of 2.11 gallons would have run out before the second coat. Measure the railing length and count the steps before you buy.

Assuming the second coat covers like the first. The second coat goes about twice as far because the wood is already sealed — 500 sq ft per gallon against 250 on the first coat for a semi-transparent stain. Budgeting both coats at the first-coat rate buys a gallon you do not need; budgeting the second coat at the first-coat rate the other way round leaves you short. Use the two rates from your can, not one.

Method and limits

The formula is total area = floor (length × width) + railing (length × 4.5 sq ft per ft for a baluster railing, or length × height × 2) + stairs (steps × width × 18.75 ÷ 12), then first coat = area ÷ first-coat coverage, second coat = area ÷ second-coat coverage, and to buy = (first coat + second coat) × 1.10, rounded up to whole cans — the same approach the Deck Stain Calculator uses. Coverage depends heavily on the wood, its age and the product: the label range is the best guide, and the low end is the safe end for old or rough boards. The railing factor of 4.5 sq ft per ft is an estimate for a standard baluster railing — measure one section of your railing if it is unusual. The $45 per gallon stain price, $17 cleaner and $1.50 per sq ft labour rate are example prices, not quotes. This guide covers the stain quantity only: it does not price the deck, the boards, the cleaner beyond the example, or the labour beyond the example rate, and it does not cover spraying versus brushing, which changes how much stain lands on the wood. Frequently asked questions

How many gallons of stain do I need for a 12x16 deck?

Floor only, two coats on smooth wood: 1.27 gallons, so buy 2 one-gallon cans. With 36 ft of railing and 3 steps added, the same deck is 372.75 sq ft and needs 2.46 gallons — buy 3 cans. Railings and stairs are where most decks run short, so count them before you buy.

How much does a gallon of deck stain cover?

About 150 to 300 sq ft per gallon on the first coat on smooth wood — a semi-transparent stain covers about 250 to 300 sq ft per gallon — and roughly twice as far on the second coat, about 500 to 600 sq ft per gallon. Rough or weathered wood soaks up more, about 150 sq ft per gallon on the first coat. The number on your can is the authority for your product.

Do I need one or two coats of deck stain?

Check the label: transparent and solid stains are often one coat, while semi-transparent acrylics — for example Behr DeckPlus — need two thin coats for the look and durability they are rated for. A second coat also evens out the colour, so budget for it unless the label says one coat is enough.

How do I calculate stain for deck railings?

A baluster railing has far more surface than its outline, so allow about 4.5 sq ft per linear ft of railing, or measure length times height times 2 for both faces. On the worked deck in this guide, 48 ft of railing adds 216 sq ft — nearly as much as the 320 sq ft floor — so skipping the railing is the classic way to run out of stain halfway.

How much stain do I need for deck stairs?

Each step is a tread (11.25 in) plus a riser (7.5 in): steps times width times 18.75 divided by 12 gives the stair area in sq ft. Five 4 ft steps add 31.25 sq ft. Add the stairs to the floor and railing area before you divide by the coverage.

How much does it cost to stain a deck?

At the example prices in this guide — $45 per gallon of stain and $17 for cleaner — the 16 ft by 20 ft worked deck costs about $197 in materials (4 gallons plus cleaner). A professional job at the example $1.50 per sq ft labour rate brings the same deck to about $1,050 all in. These are example prices, not a quote, so get a price from your local supplier before you buy.
