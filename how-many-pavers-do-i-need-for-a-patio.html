How Many Pavers Do I Need for a Patio?

One real patio, counted layer by layer — pavers, base gravel, bedding sand, joint sand and edge restraint — so your order list is complete before you call the supplier.

Short answer

A 16 ft × 12 ft patio needs 212 pavers of 12×12 in size, or 951 of 4×8 in brick pavers, once a 10% waste allowance is added. Under them go 2.37 cubic yards of compacted base gravel, 32 bags of 50 lb bedding sand, 2 to 3 bags of polymeric joint sand, and 56 linear ft of edge restraint. The count comes from one rule: divide the patio area by the area of one paver, then add waste for cuts and breakage. Try the Paver Calculator or the Brick Paver Calculator for your own patio, or follow the walkthrough below.

The formula

Area = patio length × patio width, in square feet. Paver area = paver length × paver width ÷ 144, in square feet (144 is the number of square inches in a square foot). Pavers = area ÷ paver area, rounded up. Pavers to buy = pavers × 1.10 (10% waste), rounded up. Base gravel = area × base depth in feet ÷ 27, in cubic yards. Bedding sand = area × 1/12 ÷ 27, in cubic yards, or area × 1/12 in cubic feet ÷ 0.5 for 50 lb bags. Edge restraint = 2 × (length + width), in linear feet. The count uses the paver's own area; the narrow 1/8 in joints between pavers are small enough that the 10% waste allowance covers them.

Worked example: 16 ft × 12 ft patio, 12×12 in pavers

Step 1: the area is 16 × 12 = 192 sq ft. Step 2: one 12×12 in paver covers 12 × 12 ÷ 144 = 1 sq ft. Step 3: pavers are 192 ÷ 1 = 192. Step 4: with 10% waste, 192 × 1.10 = 211.2, rounded up to 212 pavers to buy. Step 5: base gravel at 4 in compacted is 192 × (4 ÷ 12) = 64 cu ft, and 64 ÷ 27 = 2.37 cubic yards — about 3.3 tons at roughly 1.4 tons per cubic yard. Step 6: bedding sand at 1 in is 192 × (1 ÷ 12) = 16 cu ft, which is 16 ÷ 27 = 0.59 cubic yards, or 16 ÷ 0.5 = 32 bags of 50 lb. Step 7: polymeric sand for 1/8 in joints runs about 75 to 100 sq ft per 50 lb bag, so 192 sq ft needs 2 to 3 bags. Step 8: edge restraint is 2 × (16 + 12) = 56 linear ft. As a rough materials budget, 212 pavers at about $3 each is $636, base gravel at about $45 per cubic yard is $107, bedding sand at about $6 a bag is $192, and polymeric sand at about $30 a bag is $60 to $90 — roughly $995 to $1,025 for materials, before labour. Prices vary by region and supplier, so confirm them before you order.

Same patio in 4×8 in brick pavers

Swap the paver size and only the paver count changes — the base, the bedding and the edge restraint stay exactly the same, because they depend on the area, not the paver size. One 4×8 in paver covers 4 × 8 ÷ 144 = 0.222 sq ft, so 192 sq ft needs 192 × 4.5 = 864 pavers, and with 10% waste that is 864 × 1.10 = 950.4, rounded up to 951 pavers to buy. Small pavers mean far more pieces and far more joints: polymeric sand coverage drops to about 88 sq ft per 50 lb bag at 1/8 in joints, so the same patio needs 192 ÷ 88 = 2.18, rounded up to 3 bags. Method and limits

This guide counts pavers for a rectangular patio: area divided by one paver's area, rounded up, plus a 10% waste allowance for a straight layout. It assumes one paver size, 1/8 in joints, a 4 in compacted gravel base and a 1 in bedding-sand layer for a walk-on patio. Driveways need a 6 to 8 in base, herringbone, diagonal and curved layouts need more waste than 10%, and irregular shapes, circles and borders with a second paver size need their own count. The polymeric-sand bags are a planning estimate from typical coverage rates, not a volume calculation. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final list with your supplier before you order. Frequently asked questions

How many pavers do I need for a 12x12 patio?

A 12x12 ft patio is 144 sq ft. With 12x12 in pavers (1 sq ft each) it needs 144 pavers, 159 with 10% waste. With 4x8 in brick pavers (4.5 per sq ft) it needs 648 pavers, 713 with 10% waste.

How many 4x8 pavers are in a square foot?

About 4.5. A 4x8 in paver covers 32 sq in, and a square foot is 144 sq in, so 144 divided by 32 is 4.5 pavers per sq ft. A 100 sq ft area needs about 450 of them, 495 with 10% waste.

How deep should the gravel base be under pavers?

4 inches of compacted gravel for a patio or walkway, and 6 to 8 inches for a driveway that carries vehicles. Cold-climate subgrades need more. The base, not the pavers, is what keeps the surface flat for years.

How much sand do I need under pavers?

Two sands: a 1 inch bedding layer under the pavers and polymeric sand swept into the joints. A 16x12 ft patio needs 16 cu ft of bedding sand, about 0.59 cubic yards or 32 bags of 50 lb, plus 2 to 3 bags of 50 lb polymeric sand for 1/8 in joints.

How much extra should I order for cuts and waste?

Add 5% for a straight running-bond or stack pattern on a square or rectangular patio, and 10% for herringbone, diagonal or curved layouts, which produce more cut pieces. Order the waste with the main order, because pavers vary slightly in colour between production runs.

Do I need edge restraint for a paver patio?

Yes. Without a plastic or metal edge restraint spiked into the base, the outer pavers spread and the whole field loosens from the edges in. Measure the perimeter to size it: a 16x12 ft patio needs 56 linear ft of edge restraint.
