How Much Gravel Do I Need for a Driveway?

A full driveway project, worked step by step: base layer, surface layer, tons, and the mistakes that leave a driveway short.

Short answer

A 12 ft × 30 ft driveway needs about 10.5 tons of gravel in total: 7.2 tons of crusher-run base plus 3.3 tons of surface stone, each including a 10% compaction allowance. That is one tandem-truck load for most suppliers. Work through the project below with your own size, or try the Gravel Driveway Calculator for an instant result.

The formula

Cubic feet = length × width × (depth in inches ÷ 12). Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Tons = cubic yards × tons per cubic yard for that stone, then add the compaction allowance. A shortcut for yards is: area in square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards.

Worked project: 12 ft × 30 ft driveway, two layers

Step 1: area is 12 × 30 = 360 sq ft. Step 2, the base: 4 inches of crusher run gives 360 × (4 ÷ 12) = 120 cu ft = 4.44 cu yd. At 1.48 tons per cubic yard that is 6.57 tons, and with a 10% compaction allowance it is 7.23 tons — order about 7.2 tons. Step 3, the surface: 2 inches of #57 stone gives 360 × (2 ÷ 12) = 60 cu ft = 2.22 cu yd. At 1.35 tons per cubic yard that is 3.00 tons, and with a 10% allowance it is 3.30 tons — order about 3.3 tons. Step 4, the total: 7.23 + 3.30 = 10.53 tons, so about 10.5 tons in total. Order the two stones separately: a supplier cannot split one load into base and surface stone for you.

Why two layers, not one

The base layer does the structural work: crusher run, which is stone down to dust, compacts hard when damp and spreads the weight of vehicles. The surface layer does the driving work: angular #57 stone locks together, drains, and gives a firm top to drive on. A single thick layer of rounded pea gravel would shift under tyres, and a single layer of crusher run alone would stay dusty and soft on top. Keep the layers separate in your order, and compact the base before the surface goes on.

What changes the number

Depth matters most: doubling the depth doubles the gravel. Stone type matters next: crusher run at 1.48 tons per cubic yard weighs more per yard than #57 stone at 1.35, so the same volume orders different tonnages. Soft ground, heavy vehicles and slopes all push the depth — and the tonnage — up. Always confirm the weight per cubic yard on your supplier's ticket, because density varies by quarry and moisture.

Method and limits

This guide uses the standard volume formula for a rectangular driveway: length × width × depth, converted with 27 cubic feet per cubic yard, then converted to tons with typical loose densities (1.48 tons per cubic yard for crusher run, 1.35 for #57 stone) plus a 10% compaction allowance. It assumes a flat, rectangular area measured at its widest points. Irregular shapes, slopes, soft subsoil and different stone densities change the result, so treat the total as an estimate and confirm the final quantity with your supplier. Frequently asked questions

How many tons of gravel do I need for a driveway?

It depends on size and depth. A 12 ft x 30 ft driveway with a 4 inch crusher-run base and a 2 inch surface layer needs about 10.5 tons in total: 7.2 tons of base plus 3.3 tons of surface stone, each including a 10% compaction allowance.

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

A common build is a 4 to 6 inch compacted base of crusher run topped with 1.5 to 2 inches of surface stone such as #57 stone. Soft soil or heavy vehicles need more depth, so check local guidance for your ground.

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

Most crushed stone weighs about 1.3 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard. This guide uses 1.35 tons per cubic yard for #57 surface stone and 1.48 tons per cubic yard for crusher run. Your quarry's ticket weight is the final word.

Should I order gravel in tons or cubic yards?

Order in the unit your supplier sells by, and convert before you call. Cubic yards measure volume; tons measure weight. The two are linked by the stone's density, which varies by stone type, so do not use one stone's conversion for another.

Why add extra gravel for compaction?

Loose stone settles and compacts once it is spread and driven on. Adding about 10% for a compacted base means the finished layer still reaches the depth you planned, instead of coming up short after the first weeks of use.

Is bulk delivery cheaper than bags for a driveway?

For a whole driveway, yes, almost always: bulk delivery by the ton is far cheaper per ton than bags, even after a delivery fee. Bags suit small repairs and top-ups, not a full driveway build.
