How Many Sheets of Drywall Do I Need? A Comparison Table for Every Room Size

One table for every common room — walls and ceiling, 4×8 and 4×12 sheets, walls-only counts — plus the finishing materials, so your whole order is on one page.

Short answer

A 12 ft × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, its ceiling, and one door and one window subtracted needs 17 sheets of 4×8 drywall (12 sheets of 4×12) with 10% waste — plus about 5 gallons of joint compound, 1 roll of tape and about 500 screws. Without subtracting the openings, the same room needs 19 sheets of 4×8. The table below covers every common room size, so you can read your count straight off it. Try the Drywall Calculator for your own room, or work through the formula below.

The formula

Wall area = room perimeter × wall height, where the perimeter of a rectangular room is 2 × (length + width). Ceiling area = length × width. Total board area = wall area + ceiling area − large openings (a standard door is about 20 sq ft, a window about 15 sq ft). Sheets to buy = total board area × (1 + waste %) ÷ area per sheet, rounded up — 10% waste for a plain rectangular room, 15% for a room with many openings, angles or short wall sections. A 4×8 sheet covers 32 sq ft, a 4×10 covers 40 sq ft and a 4×12 covers 48 sq ft. Drywall is sold only in whole sheets, so always round up.

Every row uses 8 ft walls and 10% waste. The walls + ceiling columns include the ceiling; the walls-only column is for rooms where the ceiling is already done or is not being covered. No openings are subtracted in this table — subtract a door and a window and most rooms drop by about one sheet.

Two things to read from the table. First, the ceiling is a large share of the order — on the 12 × 12 ft room it is 144 of the 528 sq ft, so skipping it drops the count from 19 sheets to 14. Second, 4×12 sheets cut the count by about a third, but they are heavier and need two people, so the saving is only worth it on long walls and ceilings. For a whole house, the rule of thumb is that the board area is about 3 times the floor-plan area: a 1,000 sq ft floor plan is roughly 3,000 sq ft of board, which is about 104 sheets of 4×8 with 10% waste.

Worked example: 12 ft × 12 ft room, 8 ft walls, one door and one window

Step 1: the perimeter is 2 × (12 + 12) = 48 ft, so the wall area is 48 × 8 = 384 sq ft. Step 2: subtract one door (about 20 sq ft) and one window (about 15 sq ft) to get 384 − 35 = 349 sq ft of wall. Step 3: the ceiling is 12 × 12 = 144 sq ft, so the total board area is 349 + 144 = 493 sq ft. Step 4: with 10% waste, 493 × 1.10 = 542.3 sq ft to cover. Step 5: in 4×8 sheets, 542.3 ÷ 32 = 16.95, rounded up to 17 sheets; in 4×12 sheets, 542.3 ÷ 48 = 11.3, rounded up to 12 sheets. Step 6: walls only, without the ceiling, 349 × 1.10 = 383.9 sq ft, which is 383.9 ÷ 32 = 12.0, so 12 sheets. If your own count for a room this size is far from 17 sheets, check the waste allowance first — forgetting it is the most common reason an order comes up short.

4×8, 4×10 or 4×12 sheets?

The sheet size changes the count, the weight and the number of joints you have to tape. Pick the size that fits the room and the crew, not just the lowest count.

Sheet sizeArea per sheetWeight (1/2 in)Best for
4 × 8 ft32 sq ftAbout 52 lbAlmost everything. One person can carry and hang it.
4 × 10 ft40 sq ftAbout 65 lbWalls up to 10 ft hung vertically, fewer horizontal joints.
4 × 12 ft48 sq ftAbout 78 lbLong walls and ceilings. Fewest seams, needs two people.

On a 12 ft wall, a 4×12 sheet hung horizontally runs the full length with no butt joint, which is one less joint to tape and sand. On 9 ft walls the maths changes too: the same 12 × 12 ft room with 9 ft walls is 48 × 9 = 432 sq ft of wall plus 144 sq ft of ceiling, 576 sq ft in all, which is 20 sheets of 4×8 or 14 sheets of 4×12 with 10% waste. Board thickness matters as well: 1/2 in is the usual choice for interior walls, 5/8 in fire-rated (Type X) board is commonly required on walls and ceilings between an attached garage and the living space, and moisture-resistant board is for bathrooms around tubs and showers — check your local building code before you buy. Method and limits

This guide counts drywall sheets for a rectangular room: wall area (perimeter × height) plus ceiling area, minus large openings, multiplied by 1.10 for waste, divided by the sheet area and rounded up to whole sheets. It assumes 8 ft walls unless the text says otherwise, one sheet size across the room, and measurements taken wall to wall. Irregular rooms should be split into rectangles and added, with 15% waste for the extra cuts. Waste percentages and the finishing-material rates are common planning rules of thumb from the board manufacturer's published figures, not a standard — no building code sets an overage. Treat the total as a planning estimate and confirm the final order with your supplier before you buy. Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12x12 room?

A 12x12 ft room with 8 ft walls and its ceiling is 528 sq ft of board. With 10% waste that is 19 sheets of 4x8 drywall, or 13 sheets of 4x12. Subtract one door (about 20 sq ft) and one window (about 15 sq ft) and the count drops to 17 sheets of 4x8, or 12 sheets of 4x12. Walls only, without the ceiling, need 14 sheets of 4x8 with 10% waste.

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 10x10 room?

A 10x10 ft room with 8 ft walls and its ceiling is 420 sq ft of board. With 10% waste that is 15 sheets of 4x8 drywall, or 10 sheets of 4x12. Walls only need 11 sheets of 4x8 with 10% waste.

How many 4x8 sheets of drywall do I need per 1,000 sq ft?

For 1,000 sq ft of board area, add 10% waste to get 1,100 sq ft, then divide by 32 sq ft per 4x8 sheet: 1,100 divided by 32 is 34.4, rounded up to 35 sheets. In 4x12 sheets the same area needs 23 sheets (1,100 divided by 48 is 22.9, rounded up).

Should I use 4x8 or 4x12 drywall sheets?

Use 4x8 sheets for most rooms: a 1/2 in sheet weighs about 52 lb, one person can carry and hang it, and it fits up stairs and through doorways. Use 4x12 sheets on long walls and ceilings, where they leave fewer joints to tape and no butt joint across a 12 ft wall — but a 1/2 in 4x12 sheet weighs about 78 lb, needs two people, and is hard to get up stairs or onto a ceiling alone.

How much joint compound, tape and screws do I need for a 12x12 room?

For a 12x12 ft room with 8 ft walls, ceiling, and one door and one window subtracted (493 sq ft of board), allow about 5 gallons of ready-mixed joint compound, which is 2 pails of 4.5 gallons, about 182 ft of paper tape, so 1 roll of 250 ft, and about 500 screws, roughly 32 per sheet, so one 5 lb box covers it. These are planning rates from USG's published figures: about 9.4 gallons of compound and 370 ft of tape per 1,000 sq ft of board, and about 1,000 screws per 1,000 sq ft with framing 16 in on center.

Do I subtract doors and windows when calculating drywall?

Subtract large openings: a standard door is about 20 sq ft and a window about 15 sq ft, and leaving them in over-orders by a sheet or two on a small room. Small openings can stay in the total, because the board is often hung over the opening and cut out, and the offcut is rarely usable. When in doubt, leave an opening in — a spare sheet is cheaper than a second trip to the store.
