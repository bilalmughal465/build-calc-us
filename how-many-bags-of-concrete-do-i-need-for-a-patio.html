How Many Bags of Concrete Do I Need for a Patio? 40 lb vs 60 lb vs 80 lb Comparison Table

One patio worked from measuring to ordering — the bag-size comparison, the volume and bag count for every common patio size, the gravel base, and the dollar crossover where bags stop making sense and a ready-mix truck takes over.

Short answer

A 12 ft × 12 ft patio 4 in thick needs 88 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (118 bags of 60 lb, 176 bags of 40 lb) — or 1.96 cubic yards of ready-mix. The honest headline is that a patio this size sits right on the crossover: bags are still doable, but the truck is already cheaper on material — about $440 for bags against about $294 for ready-mix — before you count the hours of mixing. Work the comparison below for your own size, or run your numbers through the Concrete Patio Calculator.

The worked example: a 12 ft × 12 ft patio, step by step

Two calculators do the two jobs in this project: the Concrete Patio Calculator for the volume and bag count, and the Gravel Patio Calculator for the base underneath. Here is the whole job by hand.

Step 1: Measure the patio. Measure the length and width in feet. A small patio is usually 10 ft × 10 ft; a comfortable family patio is 12 ft × 12 ft or 12 ft × 16 ft. If the patio is L-shaped or wraps around a corner, split it into rectangles, work each one separately, and add the volumes — one big rectangle over-counts the corner. Our example is a plain 12 ft × 12 ft square, 144 sq ft.

Step 2: Pick the thickness. 4 in is the standard thickness for a patio that only sees foot traffic and furniture; 5 to 6 in is recommended for a hot tub, an outdoor kitchen, or heavy planters. Thickness is the number that changes your order the most — going from 4 to 5 in adds a quarter to the volume. Our example is 4 in. Check your local code before you pour; the thickness table below shows what each inch costs you.

Step 3: Work out the volume. Volume = length × width × thickness in feet, and thickness in feet is thickness in inches ÷ 12. For our patio: 12 × 12 × (4 ÷ 12) = 144 × 0.33 = 48.00 cu ft. Divide by 27 for cubic yards: 48.00 ÷ 27 = 1.78 cu yd.

Step 4: Add 10% waste, then round up. Ground is never perfectly level, forms bulge a little, and some concrete always spills. Multiply by 1.10: 48.00 × 1.10 = 52.80 cu ft, or 52.80 ÷ 27 = 1.96 cu yd to order. Never round down — a part bag still has to be bought whole, and a part yard still has to be ordered.

Step 5: Divide by the bag yield. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cu ft, a 60 lb bag about 0.45 cu ft, and a 40 lb bag about 0.30 cu ft (Quikrete and Sakrete data-sheet yields). For our patio: 52.80 ÷ 0.60 = 88.0, so 88 bags of 80 lb. Before waste it would be 48.00 ÷ 0.60 = 80.0, exactly 80 bags. The other sizes: 52.80 ÷ 0.45 = 117.3, so 118 bags of 60 lb; 52.80 ÷ 0.30 = 176.0, so 176 bags of 40 lb.

Step 6: Work out the gravel base. A patio is normally poured over a compacted crushed-stone base, commonly 4 in deep. For our patio the base is the same area at 4 in: 12 × 12 × (4 ÷ 12) = 48.00 cu ft, or 1.78 cu yd. Crushed stone weighs about 2,700 lb per cu yd, so 1.78 × 2,700 = 4,806 lb, or about 2.4 tons. The Gravel Patio Calculator runs your own depth.

Step 7: Compare bags against ready-mix. At a typical US estimate of $5 per 80 lb bag, 88 × $5 = $440 for bags. At $150 per cu yd of ready-mix, 1.96 × $150 = $294 delivered. Bags cost $146 more and weigh 7,040 lb to move by hand — and that is before the mixer hire. The crossover is covered in full below, but the short version is: a patio this size is the last size where bags are still a real option.

Bags vs ready-mix: the dollar crossover

One cubic yard takes 45 bags of 80 lb, so at $5 a bag a yard of bagged concrete costs $225 before you lift a shovel — against about $150 a yard for ready-mix delivered. Bags are more expensive per yard at any size; the only question is whether the job is small enough that mixing bags is still practical. The crossover is about 1.5 to 2 cu yd: under that, a small patio or repair is a bag job; over it, the truck wins on price, time and your back. A 12 ft × 12 ft patio is 1.96 cu yd — right on the crossover — so it is the last size where bags are still a real option: $440 against $294, 7,040 lb against a truck, and a full day of mixing against an hour of pouring. A 10 ft × 10 ft patio is 1.36 cu yd and bags still make sense; a 16 ft × 16 ft patio is 3.48 cu yd and the truck wins outright — 157 bags cost about $785 against about $522 for ready-mix. The footing checklist shows the other side of the same line, where a 24 ft strip footing is only 0.59 cu yd and 30 bags win easily.

Watch the short-load fee. Many ready-mix suppliers add a short-load fee on small orders, often below about 3 to 4 cu yd — and our 1.96 cu yd sits under that line. Ask the supplier for the delivered price including any short-load fee before you decide; even with the fee, a patio order usually still beats 88 bags. Treat the $5 bag price and the $150 per-yard price as planning estimates, not quotes — prices vary by region and store, so confirm with your supplier before you buy.

Common mistakes that leave you short

The classic errors are the same ones in the other concrete guides: measuring the wrong thing, then trusting the maths. The most common mistakes are entering the thickness in inches as if it were feet (4 in entered as 4 ft orders twelve times too much concrete), forgetting the 10% waste allowance on ground that is never perfectly level, measuring one big rectangle for an L-shaped patio instead of splitting it into sections, forgetting the gravel base entirely and then having no stone on site, rounding down instead of up, and pricing bags without the mixer hire and the day of labour they need. Other common slips are mixing bag sizes without changing the yield (a 60 lb bag is not two-thirds of an 80 lb bag in volume — it is 0.45 cu ft against 0.60), buying 40 lb bags because they are lighter and then discovering you need twice as many of them, and ordering the concrete before the forms and joints are set — saw control joints about every 8 to 10 ft so cracking follows a straight line. Measure the patio, work the volume, divide by the yield, add waste, then compare with the truck price — in that order.

Method and limits

This guide calculates patio volume as length × width × depth, with depth converted from inches to feet by dividing by 12 and cubic feet converted to cubic yards by dividing by 27. Bag yields are the manufacturer data-sheet figures: 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag, 0.45 per 60 lb bag, 0.30 per 40 lb bag. A 10% waste allowance is added before rounding up to whole bags. The gravel base estimate uses about 2,700 lb of crushed stone per cu yd. The cost example uses typical US estimates of $5 per 80 lb bag, about $4.50 per 60 lb bag and about $3 per 40 lb bag, and $150 per cu yd of ready-mix — prices vary by region and store, so confirm with your supplier. This guide counts concrete and base only: it does not size the slab, choose the reinforcement, or set the thickness — thickness, reinforcement and joints are set by your local building code, soil and loads, so verify them with your contractor before you pour. Frequently asked questions

How many bags of concrete do I need for a patio?

A 12 ft × 12 ft patio 4 in thick needs 88 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (118 bags of 60 lb, 176 bags of 40 lb). Its volume is 48.00 cu ft (1.78 cu yd), or 52.80 cu ft (1.96 cu yd) with the waste allowance; at 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag that is 88 bags. At 1.96 cu yd the patio sits right on the bags vs ready-mix crossover — see the cost comparison above.

How many bags of concrete for a 10x10 patio?

A 10 ft × 10 ft patio 4 in thick needs 62 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (82 bags of 60 lb, 123 bags of 40 lb). Its volume is 33.33 cu ft (1.23 cu yd), or 36.67 cu ft (1.36 cu yd) with waste; 36.67 divided by 0.60 cu ft per bag is 61.1, rounded up to 62 bags.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete make a cubic yard?

45 bags of 80 lb concrete mix make one cubic yard, because a cubic yard is 27 cu ft and each 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cu ft (27 divided by 0.60 = 45). A cubic yard takes 60 bags of 60 lb mix (0.45 cu ft each) or 90 bags of 40 lb mix (0.30 cu ft each), before waste.

Should I use bags or ready-mix concrete for a patio?

It depends on the size. Under about 1.5 cu yd, bags are usually the practical choice; over about 2 cu yd, ready-mix is almost always cheaper and far less work. A 12 ft × 12 ft patio is 1.96 cu yd — right on the crossover — where 88 bags of 80 lb cost about $440 at $5 a bag against about $294 for ready-mix at $150 per cu yd, but the truck may add a short-load fee on orders under about 3 to 4 cu yd, so compare the delivered price before you decide.

How thick should a concrete patio be?

Thickness is the number that changes your order the most — going from 4 to 5 in on a 12 ft × 12 ft patio adds 25% to the volume, from 88 bags to 110 bags of 80 lb. Your local code and soil decide the final figure, so check with your contractor.

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 16x16 patio?

A 16 ft × 16 ft patio 4 in thick needs 157 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (209 bags of 60 lb, 313 bags of 40 lb). Its volume is 85.33 cu ft (3.16 cu yd), or 93.87 cu ft (3.48 cu yd) with waste. At that size ready-mix is the clear choice — 157 bags weigh 12,560 lb and cost about $785 at $5 a bag, against about $522 for 3.48 cu yd of ready-mix at $150 per cu yd.
