How Many Bags of Concrete Do I Need for a Walkway? A Worked Project Walkthrough

One walkway worked from measuring to ordering — the volume and bag count for every common walkway length, the gravel base, and the dollar crossover where bags stop making sense and a ready-mix truck takes over.

Short answer

A 3 ft × 30 ft walkway 4 in thick needs 55 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (74 bags of 60 lb, 110 bags of 40 lb) — or 1.22 cubic yards of ready-mix. A walkway is the classic small bag job: long and narrow, so the volume stays small even at 30 ft, and at 1.22 cu yd it sits comfortably under the bags vs ready-mix crossover. Work the walkthrough below for your own length, or run your numbers through the Concrete Walkway Calculator.

The worked example: a 3 ft × 30 ft walkway, step by step

Two calculators do the two jobs in this project: the Concrete Walkway Calculator for the volume and bag count, and the Gravel Path Calculator for the base underneath. Here is the whole job by hand.

Step 1: Measure the walkway. Measure the length in feet, then the width. A garden path is usually 3 ft wide; a front walk that two people use side by side is 4 ft wide. If the walkway bends or has a landing pad at the door, split it into rectangles, work each one separately, and add the volumes — one big rectangle over-counts the corner. Our example is a plain 3 ft × 30 ft straight run, 90 sq ft.

Step 2: Pick the thickness. 4 in is the standard thickness for a walkway that only sees foot traffic; 5 to 6 in is recommended where vehicles cross it, for example a walkway that doubles as a driveway apron. Thickness is the number that changes your order the most — going from 4 to 5 in adds a quarter to the volume. Our example is 4 in. Check your local code before you pour; the thickness table below shows what each inch costs you.

Step 3: Work out the volume. Volume = length × width × thickness in feet, and thickness in feet is thickness in inches ÷ 12. For our walkway: 3 × 30 × (4 ÷ 12) = 90 × 0.33 = 30.00 cu ft. Divide by 27 for cubic yards: 30.00 ÷ 27 = 1.11 cu yd.

Step 4: Add 10% waste, then round up. Ground is never perfectly level, forms bulge a little, and some concrete always spills. Multiply by 1.10: 30.00 × 1.10 = 33.00 cu ft, or 33.00 ÷ 27 = 1.22 cu yd to order. Never round down — a part bag still has to be bought whole, and a part yard still has to be ordered.

Step 5: Divide by the bag yield. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cu ft, a 60 lb bag about 0.45 cu ft, and a 40 lb bag about 0.30 cu ft (Quikrete and Sakrete data-sheet yields). For our walkway: 33.00 ÷ 0.60 = 55.0, so 55 bags of 80 lb. Before waste it would be 30.00 ÷ 0.60 = 50.0, exactly 50 bags. The other sizes: 33.00 ÷ 0.45 = 73.3, so 74 bags of 60 lb; 33.00 ÷ 0.30 = 110.0, so 110 bags of 40 lb.

Step 6: Work out the gravel base. A walkway is normally poured over a compacted crushed-stone base, commonly 4 in deep. For our walkway the base is the same area at 4 in: 3 × 30 × (4 ÷ 12) = 30.00 cu ft, or 1.11 cu yd. Crushed stone weighs about 2,700 lb per cu yd, so 1.11 × 2,700 = 2,997 lb, or about 1.5 tons. The Gravel Path Calculator runs your own depth.

Step 7: Compare bags against ready-mix. At a typical US estimate of $5 per 80 lb bag, 55 × $5 = $275 for bags. At $150 per cu yd of ready-mix, 1.22 × $150 = $183 delivered. Bags cost $92 more and weigh 4,400 lb to move by hand — and that is before the mixer hire. The crossover is covered in full below, but the short version is: a walkway this size is still a bag job, because the truck's short-load fee usually eats the saving on a 1.22 cu yd order.

Bags vs ready-mix: the dollar crossover

One cubic yard takes 45 bags of 80 lb, so at $5 a bag a yard of bagged concrete costs $225 before you lift a shovel — against about $150 a yard for ready-mix delivered. Bags are more expensive per yard at any size; the only question is whether the job is small enough that mixing bags is still practical. The crossover is about 1.5 to 2 cu yd: under that, a small walkway or repair is a bag job; over it, the truck wins on price, time and your back. A 3 ft × 30 ft walkway is 1.22 cu yd — still a bag job — so it is the last size where bags are the easy call: $275 against $183, 4,400 lb against a truck, and a day of mixing against an hour of pouring. A 3 ft × 40 ft walkway is 1.63 cu yd and sits on the crossover — 74 bags cost about $370 against about $244 for ready-mix — and a 4 ft × 40 ft walkway is 2.17 cu yd, where the truck wins outright. The footing checklist shows the other side of the same line, where a 24 ft strip footing is only 0.59 cu yd and 30 bags win easily.

Watch the short-load fee. Many ready-mix suppliers add a short-load fee on small orders, often below about 3 to 4 cu yd — and our 1.22 cu yd sits under that line. Ask the supplier for the delivered price including any short-load fee before you decide; on a walkway order the fee usually wipes out the truck's price advantage, which is why bags still win here. Treat the $5 bag price and the $150 per-yard price as planning estimates, not quotes — prices vary by region and store, so confirm with your supplier before you buy.

Common mistakes that leave you short

The classic errors are the same ones in the other concrete guides: measuring the wrong thing, then trusting the maths. The most common mistakes are entering the thickness in inches as if it were feet (4 in entered as 4 ft orders twelve times too much concrete), forgetting the 10% waste allowance on ground that is never perfectly level, measuring one big rectangle for a walkway with a bend or a landing pad instead of splitting it into sections, forgetting the gravel base entirely and then having no stone on site, rounding down instead of up, and pricing bags without the mixer hire and the day of labour they need. Other common slips are mixing bag sizes without changing the yield (a 60 lb bag is not two-thirds of an 80 lb bag in volume — it is 0.45 cu ft against 0.60), buying 40 lb bags because they are lighter and then discovering you need twice as many of them, and pouring the walkway in one long slab with no control joints — saw joints about every 8 to 10 ft so cracking follows a straight line. Measure the walkway, work the volume, divide by the yield, add waste, then compare with the truck price — in that order.

Method and limits

This guide calculates walkway volume as length × width × depth, with depth converted from inches to feet by dividing by 12 and cubic feet converted to cubic yards by dividing by 27. Bag yields are the manufacturer data-sheet figures: 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag, 0.45 per 60 lb bag, 0.30 per 40 lb bag. A 10% waste allowance is added before rounding up to whole bags. The gravel base estimate uses about 2,700 lb of crushed stone per cu yd. The cost example uses typical US estimates of $5 per 80 lb bag, about $4.50 per 60 lb bag and about $3 per 40 lb bag, and $150 per cu yd of ready-mix — prices vary by region and store, so confirm with your supplier. This guide counts concrete and base only: it does not size the slab, choose the reinforcement, or set the thickness — thickness, reinforcement and joints are set by your local building code, soil and loads, so verify them with your contractor before you pour. Frequently asked questions

How many bags of concrete do I need for a walkway?

A 3 ft x 30 ft walkway 4 in thick needs 55 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (74 bags of 60 lb, 110 bags of 40 lb). Its volume is 30.00 cu ft (1.11 cu yd), or 33.00 cu ft (1.22 cu yd) with the waste allowance; at 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag that is 55 bags. At 1.22 cu yd the walkway is still a bag job — under the 1.5 to 2 cu yd crossover where ready-mix takes over.

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 20 ft walkway?

A 3 ft wide, 20 ft long walkway 4 in thick needs 37 bags of 80 lb concrete mix with 10% waste (49 bags of 60 lb, 74 bags of 40 lb). Its volume is 20.00 cu ft (0.74 cu yd), or 22.00 cu ft (0.81 cu yd) with waste; 22.00 divided by 0.60 cu ft per bag is 36.7, rounded up to 37 bags.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete make a cubic yard?

45 bags of 80 lb concrete mix make one cubic yard, because a cubic yard is 27 cu ft and each 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cu ft (27 divided by 0.60 = 45). A cubic yard takes 60 bags of 60 lb mix (0.45 cu ft each) or 90 bags of 40 lb mix (0.30 cu ft each), before waste.

How thick should a concrete walkway be?

4 in is the standard thickness for a walkway that only sees foot traffic. Go to 5 to 6 in if vehicles will cross it, for example a walkway that doubles as a driveway apron. Thickness is the number that changes your order the most — going from 4 to 5 in on a 3 ft x 30 ft walkway adds 25% to the volume, from 55 bags to 69 bags of 80 lb. Your local code and soil decide the final figure, so check with your contractor.

Should I use bags or ready-mix concrete for a walkway?

It depends on the length. Under about 1.5 cu yd, bags are usually the practical choice; over about 2 cu yd, ready-mix is almost always cheaper and far less work. A 3 ft x 30 ft walkway is 1.22 cu yd — still a bag job — where 55 bags of 80 lb cost about $275 at $5 a bag against about $183 for ready-mix at $150 per cu yd, but the truck may add a short-load fee on orders under about 3 to 4 cu yd, so compare the delivered price before you decide.

How much gravel do I need under a concrete walkway?

A 3 ft x 30 ft walkway over a 4 in compacted crushed-stone base needs about 1.11 cu yd of gravel, or about 1.5 tons. The base is the same area at the same depth: 3 x 30 x (4 divided by 12) = 30.00 cu ft, or 1.11 cu yd, and crushed stone weighs about 2,700 lb per cu yd, so 1.11 x 2,700 = about 3,000 lb.
