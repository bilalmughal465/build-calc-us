How Many Bricks Do I Need for a Fire Pit? Round vs Square Comparison Table

A fire pit is a ring, not a wall — so you count rings and courses, not just square feet. The full count for a 3 ft round pit, the round-vs-square comparison, the size tables, the firebrick inner ring, and the two mistakes that leave a pit half-built.

Short answer

A round fire pit 3 ft across inside and 18 in high needs 117 modular bricks to order — 106 net plus 10% waste. That is about 16 bricks per ring and 7 courses, plus about 1 cu ft of mortar (4 bags of 80 lb premixed mix). At the example price of $0.55 per brick, the brick costs about $64. Count your own pit with the Brick Calculator — its fire-pit mode works out the face area from the inside diameter and height. The Fire Pit Calculator sizes the ring itself. The full construction calculators list covers walls, blocks and pavers too.

The formula

Centerline diameter = inside diameter + brick depth (4 in for modular brick)

Face area (sq ft) = π × centerline diameter in feet × height in feet

Bricks net = face area × 6.75 for modular brick with 3/8 in joints (Brick Industry Association, Technical Note 10)

To order = bricks net × 1.10 (10% extra for cuts, breakage and the round ring)

Mortar (cu ft) = bricks net × 8.1 ÷ 1,000 × 1.20 (20% mortar waste)

Three steps, in this order. First, measure on the ring's centerline, not the inside diameter — the bricks sit on a circle wider than the inside of the pit, and counting the inside diameter under-orders by about 10%. Second, work out the face area of the ring and multiply by the bricks per square foot for your brick size: 6.75 for modular, 6.55 for standard non-modular, 4.5 for Norman. Third, add 10% waste — a round ring needs more cut bricks than a straight wall, so the Brick Industry Association's 5% minimum is not enough here. The course method cross-checks the area method: bricks per ring times the number of courses should land within a few bricks of the area count.

The worked example: a 3 ft round pit, 18 in high

A round pit, 3 ft across inside, 18 in high, one wythe of modular brick (nominal 4 × 2⅔ × 8 in), 3/8 in joints, 10% waste.

Step 1 — Centerline diameter. Inside diameter 36 in plus one brick depth of 4 in = 40 in, or 3.33 ft. The ring's centerline is 3 ft 4 in across.

Step 2 — Face area. π × 3.33 ft × 1.5 ft = 15.71 sq ft of brick face.

Step 3 — Bricks net. 15.71 × 6.75 modular bricks per sq ft = 106 bricks net.

Step 4 — To order. Add 10% waste: 106 × 1.10 = 116.6, so order 117 bricks.

Step 5 — Course cross-check. Centerline circumference = π × 40 in = 125.7 in. Modular brick is 8 in nominal, so 125.7 ÷ 8 = 15.7, or 16 bricks per ring. Height 18 in ÷ 2⅔ in per course = 6.75, or 7 courses. 16 × 7 = 112 bricks — within 6 of the area method's 106, and the 117 you order covers both counts.

Step 6 — Mortar. 106 bricks × 8.1 cu ft per 1,000 bricks = 0.86 cu ft, plus 20% waste = about 1 cu ft. At up to 37 standard bricks per 80 lb bag of premixed mortar mix, 117 ÷ 37 = 3.2, so buy 4 bags. Buy one more than the maths says — mortar stiffens and drops faster than brick breaks.

Step 7 — Price it. At the example price of $0.55 per brick, 117 bricks cost $64.35, about $64. Mortar is extra, and so is the firebrick inner ring below. These are example prices, not a quote — get a price from your local supplier before you order.

Cross-check with a bigger pit: a 5 ft round pit 18 in high. Centerline diameter 60 + 4 = 64 in = 5.33 ft. Face area = π × 5.33 × 1.5 = 25.13 sq ft. 25.13 × 6.75 = 170 bricks net, plus 10% = 187 to order. Run your own pit through the Brick Calculator for the exact order.

Round vs square: the same fire, fewer bricks

A circle has the shortest perimeter for a given area, so a round pit always uses fewer bricks than a square pit holding the same fire. The saving is real but modest — about a tenth — and a square pit is easier to lay, because every course is straight and the corners are the only cuts.

The square pit's extra bricks go into its corners: four corners per ring, each needing a cut or a half brick to keep the bond. A round pit has no corners at all, which is also why it needs the 10% waste allowance — the curve forces small cuts all the way round. Brick sizes: the count changes with the brick

Modular brick is the most common size in the US, but it is not the only one. Bigger bricks cover more face per brick, so they cut the count — and the mortar per brick rises with the size, so the mortar bags do not fall as fast as the brick count.

The firebrick inner ring

For a wood-burning pit, the inside face needs firebrick, not ordinary clay brick. Ordinary brick can crack and spall under direct fire heat; firebrick is made for it and is laid in refractory mortar, not ordinary mortar.

How many firebricks. Firebrick laid flat (9 × 4½ × 2½ in, 1/8 in joints) covers about 3.4 bricks per square foot of inner face. The inner face of a 3 ft pit 18 in high is π × 3 ft × 1.5 ft = 14.14 sq ft, so 14.14 × 3.4 = 48 firebricks. A 4 ft pit needs about 64, a 5 ft pit about 80. Count the firebrick separately from the outer brick — it is a different material, a different size, and a different price.

How it goes together. The firebrick ring sits inside the clay-brick ring, one brick thick, with refractory mortar in the joints. The outer ring carries the structure; the inner ring takes the heat. Leave a small gap between the two rings rather than mortaring them tight together — the firebrick expands more when hot, and a tight joint cracks the outer ring. A gas pit with a burner ring does not need a firebrick lining, because the flame does not sit against the brick the way a wood fire does.

Two mistakes that leave a pit half-built

Counting one ring instead of the whole pit. Many guides quote "80 bricks for a 3 ft fire pit" without saying how high. One ring of a 3 ft pit is only about 16 bricks — 80 bricks is roughly 5 courses, not the whole pit. An 18 in pit is 7 courses and needs 117 bricks to order. Always multiply the ring by the courses, and check the height the number assumes before you trust it.

Counting the inside diameter instead of the centerline. The bricks sit on a circle one brick-depth wider than the inside of the pit. For a 3 ft pit that is 40 in, not 36 in — and counting 36 in under-orders by about 10%, which is exactly the waste allowance, so the two errors cancel in the worst way: you run out of brick with no spare left. Measure the centerline, then add the waste on top.

Method and limits

The formula is centerline diameter = inside diameter + brick depth (4 in for modular brick), face area = π × centerline diameter in feet × height in feet, bricks net = face area × 6.75 for modular brick with 3/8 in joints, to order = bricks net × 1.10, and mortar = bricks net × 8.1 ÷ 1,000 × 1.20 — the same approach the Brick Calculator uses, from the Brick Industry Association Technical Note 10, Table 4. The 18 in height is a planning choice: 7 courses of modular brick. Taller pits scale with the face area, so a 24 in pit of the same diameter needs a third more brick. The brick counts are for a single-wythe ring in running bond; a double-walled pit roughly doubles the outer brick. The $0.55 per brick figure is an example price, not a quote. This guide covers the brick and mortar quantity only: it does not cover the footing, drainage, local fire codes, burn bans, or setback rules, which vary by town — check those with your local building or fire department before you build. Frequently asked questions

How many bricks do I need for a 3-foot fire pit?

A round fire pit 3 ft across inside and 18 in high needs 117 modular bricks to order — 106 net plus 10% waste. That is about 16 bricks per ring and 7 courses. Count the rings, not just one ring: one ring of a 3 ft pit is only about 16 bricks, so a guide that quotes 80 bricks without a height is describing roughly 5 courses, not the whole pit.

Does a round or square fire pit use fewer bricks?

A round pit uses fewer bricks for the same fire area, because a circle has the shortest perimeter for a given area. A 4 ft round pit (12.6 sq ft of fire area) needs 152 bricks to order at 18 in high, while a square pit with the same fire area needs about 170 — roughly 11% more. A square pit is easier to lay, because every course is straight, so the saving is a trade-off against easier corners.

What size brick should I use for a fire pit?

Modular brick, nominally 4 by 2 and two-thirds by 8 inches, is the most common size in the US and the one this guide counts: 6.75 bricks per square foot of wall face with 3/8 in joints, per the Brick Industry Association. Standard non-modular brick (3 and five-eighths by 2 and a quarter by 8 inches) is slightly smaller and counts 6.55 per square foot. Check the size on the label of the brick you buy — the count changes with the size.

Do I need firebrick inside a fire pit?

Yes, for a wood-burning pit. Line the inside with firebrick laid in refractory mortar: a 3 ft pit 18 in high needs about 48 firebricks for the inner ring. Ordinary clay brick can crack and spall under direct fire heat, so it belongs on the outside face, not against the fire. A gas pit with a burner ring does not need a firebrick lining.

How much mortar do I need for a fire pit?

About 1 cubic foot of mortar for a 3 ft round pit 18 in high — 106 bricks at 8.1 cubic feet per 1,000 bricks, plus 20% waste. In premixed bags that is 4 bags of 80 lb mortar mix, at up to 37 standard bricks per bag. Buy one bag more than the maths says: mortar stiffens, drops and gets wasted faster than brick does.

How high should a brick fire pit be?

Most backyard brick fire pits are 12 to 18 inches high above the ground — about 5 to 7 courses of modular brick. Eighteen inches (7 courses) is a comfortable sitting height and keeps the fire contained, while 12 inches (5 courses) suits a low pit. Taller than 18 inches starts to block the view of the fire from a chair.

How much does a brick fire pit cost?

At the example price of $0.55 per brick, a 3 ft round pit 18 in high costs about $64 of brick (117 bricks) plus mortar — 4 bags of 80 lb premixed mortar mix. Firebrick for the inner ring costs extra and varies by supplier, so get a price for it separately. These are example prices, not a quote, so check your local supplier before you order.
