How Much Sand Do I Need for Pavers? A 12x16 ft Patio Walkthrough

One paver patio worked from measuring to ordering — the bedding sand, the gravel base under it, the pavers that sit on it and the joint sand that locks them, with every number shown so you can check it against your own patio.

Short answer

A 12 ft × 16 ft patio needs 0.71 cubic yards of bedding sand to order — about 1.0 ton, or 39 bags of 50 lb sand — with a 20% compaction allowance. The finished volume is only 0.59 cubic yards; the extra 20% is the loose sand that settles when the pavers are vibrated in. The full job is bigger than the sand: the same patio also needs 2.73 cubic yards of base gravel (about 3.7 tons of #57 stone), 951 pavers of 4 × 8 in size, and 3 bags of polymeric joint sand. Work the steps below for your own size, or run your numbers through the Sand Calculator.

The worked example: a 12 ft × 16 ft paver patio, step by step

Three calculators do the three jobs in this project: the Sand Calculator for the bedding sand, the Gravel Patio Calculator for the base stone, and the Paver Calculator for the pavers that sit on them. Here is the whole job by hand.

Step 1: Measure the patio. Measure the length and width in feet, then add about 6 in on each side — the base and the sand should extend past the pavers so the edge row has something solid to sit on. Our example is a plain 12 ft × 16 ft rectangle, 192 sq ft. If the patio is L-shaped, split it into rectangles, work each one separately, and add the volumes.

Step 2: Work out how deep to dig. Add up the layers: a 4 in compacted gravel base, plus 1 in of bedding sand, plus a 2 3/8 in paver gives a dig of about 7 3/8 in below the finished height. That dig produces spoil: 192 × (7.375 ÷ 12) = 118.00 cu ft, or 4.37 cu yd of soil to move — more once it is loose. Plan where it goes before the gravel arrives. Set a gentle fall of about 1/4 in per foot away from the house for drainage, set in the aggregate layer, not the sand.

Step 3: Work out the gravel base. The base is 4 in of compacted stone on average, well-drained ground — the CMHA minimum for a patio (formerly ICPI Tech Spec 2). Volume = length × width × depth in feet: 192 × (4 ÷ 12) = 64.00 cu ft, or 2.37 cu yd finished. Add the 15% compaction allowance for a hand-tamped patio base: 2.37 × 1.15 = 2.73 cu yd to order. As #57 stone at 2,700 lb per cubic yard, that is 2.73 × 1.35 = about 3.7 tons, or 2.73 × 27 = 73.71 cu ft, divided by 0.5 = 148 bags of 50 lb stone. The patio gravel base guide shows the same maths for every patio size and compares #57 stone against crusher run.

Step 4: Work out the bedding sand. The pavers sit on 1 in of coarse concrete sand (ASTM C33) screeded level over the compacted base. Volume = 192 × (1 ÷ 12) = 16.00 cu ft, or 0.59 cu yd finished. Sand settles as the pavers are vibrated in, so add 20%: 0.59 × 1.20 = 0.71 cu yd to order. Dry sand weighs about 2,700 lb per cubic yard (1.35 tons), so 0.71 × 1.35 = about 1.0 ton. As 50 lb bags at 0.5 cu ft per bag, the finished volume is 16.00 ÷ 0.5 = 32 bags, and with the 20% allowance it is 19.20 ÷ 0.5 = 38.4, so 39 bags. Never round down — a part bag still has to be bought whole.

Step 5: Work out the pavers. A 4 × 8 in Holland paver covers 32 sq in, so there are 144 ÷ 32 = 4.5 pavers per sq ft. For our patio: 192 × 4.5 = 864 pavers before cuts. Add 10% for cutting and breakage: 864 × 1.10 = 950.4, so 951 pavers to order. The pavers walkthrough works the same count for a 16 ft × 12 ft patio and for 12 × 12 in pavers.

Step 6: Work out the joint sand. Bedding sand and joint sand are different products — do not count one as the other. Polymeric joint sand is swept dry into the joints after the pavers are laid, then watered so its binder hardens and locks the joints. One 50 lb bag covers roughly 80 to 100 sq ft of standard pavers with narrow (1/8 in) joints, so 192 ÷ 90 = 2.1, rounded up to 3 bags. Wider joints need more: at 1/4 in joints a bag covers about 60 to 80 sq ft, and irregular flagstone with 1/2 in joints can eat a bag every 20 to 30 sq ft.

Step 7: Compare bulk against bagged. The same 0.71 cubic yards of sand as bagged 50 lb sand is 39 bags — and at big-box prices bagged sand costs many times more per ton than bulk delivery. A bulk ton of sand is a typical US estimate of about $40, while 39 bags at roughly $5 each is about $195. For any patio over about 100 sq ft, bulk delivery from a landscape supply yard is the practical choice. Confirm prices with your local supplier before you order.

Step 8: The order checklist. For this patio, the order is: 2.73 cu yd (3.7 tons) of #57 stone or crusher run for the base, 0.71 cu yd (1.0 ton, or 39 bags) of ASTM C33 bedding sand, 951 pavers of 4 × 8 in, 3 bags of polymeric joint sand, and edge restraint around the full 56 ft perimeter. Order the base and the sand together from the same yard and you save a second delivery fee.

Which sand: concrete sand, mason sand, play sand or polymeric sand

Not all sand is the same, and the wrong bag is the most common reason a paver patio goes soft. Concrete sand — coarse, washed, sharp-edged, graded to ASTM C33 — is the bedding sand: its angular grains lock together under the screed and drain freely. Mason sand is finer and more uniform, which is exactly why it works for mortar but is a poor choice for bedding — it stays soft and shifts underfoot. Play sand is fine, rounded and washed soft, so it never locks up at all; pavers set on it stay spongy and rock loose. Polymeric sand is a different product again: graded sand blended with a binder that sets when watered, swept into the joints between laid pavers, never spread under them. Buy bedding sand and joint sand as two separate lines on the order, and check the ticket says concrete sand (ASTM C33) for the bedding layer.

Common mistakes that leave a paver patio wavy

The classic errors are the same ones in the other patio guides: ordering the finished sand volume without the 20% allowance and running short halfway across the patio, making the sand bed thicker to save on gravel — a thick sand bed moves under load and the pavers sink in ruts — and using play sand or mason sand for bedding because it was the bag on the shelf. Other common slips are forgetting the joint sand entirely and having no polymeric sand on site when the pavers are laid, compacting the full 4 in base in one layer instead of 2 to 3 in lifts, stopping the base at the paver edge so the edge restraint has nothing firm to sit on, and skipping the drainage fall and then watching water pool against the house. Measure the patio, pick the base depth for your soil, work the base volume, add the allowances, convert to tons with the right density — in that order.

Method and limits

This guide calculates bedding sand volume as area × depth, with depth converted from inches to feet by dividing by 12 and cubic feet converted to cubic yards by dividing by 27. A 20% compaction allowance is added for bedding sand before rounding up to the nearest whole bag. Dry sand weighs about 2,700 lb per cubic yard (1.35 tons) and damp sand about 3,000 lb (1.50 tons) — sand holds water, so a wet load reads heavy on the scale ticket, and published densities vary, so confirm the density with your supplier before you order by weight. Bagged sand is 50 lb bags at 0.5 cu ft per bag. The gravel base uses a 15% compaction allowance for a hand-tamped patio base, per the patio gravel base guide. Depth guidance follows the CMHA standard (formerly ICPI Tech Spec 2): a 4 to 6 in compacted aggregate base for patios and walkways, 6 to 8 in for residential driveways, with 1 to 1 1/2 in of ASTM C33 bedding sand screeded level. The cost example uses typical US estimates of about $40 per ton for bulk sand and about $5 per 50 lb bag — prices vary by region, supplier and haul distance, so confirm with your supplier. This guide counts base stone, bedding sand, pavers and joint sand only: it does not choose the edge restraint, set the drainage fall or check the subgrade — depth, drainage and subgrade preparation are set by your soil, climate and local code, so verify them with your contractor before you dig. Frequently asked questions

How much sand do I need for a 12x16 paver patio?

A 12 ft x 16 ft patio (192 sq ft) needs 0.59 cubic yards of bedding sand as finished volume, or 0.71 cubic yards to order with a 20% compaction allowance — about 1.0 ton of dry sand, or 39 bags of 50 lb sand at 0.5 cu ft per bag. The volume is 192 x (1 divided by 12) = 16.00 cu ft, divided by 27 = 0.59 cu yd, times 1.20 = 0.71 cu yd to order.

How much sand do I need for a 10x10 paver patio?

A 10 ft x 10 ft patio (100 sq ft) needs 0.31 cubic yards of bedding sand as finished volume, or 0.37 cubic yards to order with a 20% compaction allowance — about 0.5 tons, or 20 bags of 50 lb sand. Its volume is 100 x (1 divided by 12) = 8.33 cu ft, divided by 27 = 0.31 cu yd, times 1.20 = 0.37 cu yd to order.

How deep should the sand be under pavers?

1 in of coarse concrete sand (ASTM C33), screeded level, under the pavers — never thicker. The ICPI (now CMHA) standard is 1 to 1 1/2 in of bedding sand over a 4 to 6 in compacted gravel base. A thicker sand bed moves under load and the pavers sink in ruts, so fix low spots in the gravel base, not in the sand.

Can I use play sand under pavers?

No. Play sand is fine, rounded and washed soft, so it never locks up under a screed and the pavers stay spongy and rock loose. Mason sand is also too fine for bedding — it is made for mortar. Use coarse, washed concrete sand (ASTM C33) with sharp, angular grains that lock together and drain.

What is the difference between bedding sand and joint sand?

Bedding sand is the 1 in layer of concrete sand under the pavers that you screed level and set the pavers into. Joint sand goes between the pavers after they are laid — usually polymeric sand, which has a binder that hardens when watered and locks the joints, sheds water and keeps weeds and ants out. They are different products: one 50 lb bag of polymeric sand covers roughly 80 to 100 sq ft of standard pavers with narrow joints.

Can I use sand instead of gravel for a paver base?

No. Sand on its own does not lock together and drain the way a graded crushed stone does, so a sand-only base lets pavers rock and sink. The sand layer is there for setting the pavers to height, not for strength. Every paver maker and the ICPI/CMHA standard specify a compacted aggregate base — 4 to 6 in for a patio — under the 1 in of bedding sand.
